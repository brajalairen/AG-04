# Field advisories for AG-04 users

**Status: DRAFT.** Not yet reviewed by an agronomist. Every item cites its source; the S-numbers are in `SOURCES.md` §2. The structured version, with the full text, is `advisory.json`.

This file gives no pesticide or fungicide names or doses. Chemical control is decided by the Department of Agriculture or the KVK, and only as per the label claim approved by the Registration Committee (S1, S2).

## 1. How to read an AG-04 risk level

- **A high risk level is an early warning, not a diagnosis.** It means weather, satellite and pest-report indicators suggest conditions that favour a pest or disease. It does not confirm the pest or disease is present. *(AG-04 project policy)*
- **Satellite NDVI shows vegetation vigour, not a disease.** A drop in NDVI can come from pests, disease, water stress, harvest or cloud. Inspect the field to find out which. *(AG-04 project policy)*
- **Keep the uncertainty with the number.** Quote the confidence level and any PLACEHOLDER or SAMPLE label together with the score. A score from PLACEHOLDER thresholds is a prototype value, not a validated finding. *(AG-04 project policy)*
- **The final assessment rests with the Department of Agriculture.** *(AG-04 project policy)*

## 2. General practice

- **Monitor fields regularly.** Give more attention to areas the dashboard ranks high and to areas whose vegetation looks abnormally stressed. *(AG-04 project policy; S1 asks officials to monitor incidence regularly)*
- **Report anything suspicious early.** Unusual lesions, patches of yellowing or drying, or many insects at the plant base should go to the local Department of Agriculture office or KVK before any control measure is taken. *(S1; S6)*

## 3. Rice blast (*Magnaporthe oryzae*)

- **Look for:**
  - spindle-shaped leaf lesions with grey centres and dark borders;
  - greyish-brown neck lesions;
  - white or empty panicles. *(S4, S5)*

  Blast is easily confused with brown spot, and neck blast with stem borer damage. *(S4, S5)*
- **Weather that favours it:** "low temperatures (22-28°C), high relative humidity (>95%), dew deposits, leaf wetness for more than 10 hours". *(S1)*
- **Prevention:**
  - use certified disease-free seed and resistant varieties;
  - remove infected debris and rotate with non-host crops;
  - keep the recommended water level;
  - split nitrogen applications. *(S1, S4)*

## 4. Brown planthopper (*Nilaparvata lugens*)

- **Look for:** hopperburn, which is circular yellow patches that turn brown and dry. The insects sit at the base of the tillers, and sooty mould forms there. *(S2, S6, S9)*
- **How to scout:**
  - look daily in the seedbed and weekly in the field;
  - bend the plant slightly and tap its base to see whether planthoppers fall onto the water;
  - there is no need to scout beyond the milk stage. *(S6)*
- **When counts call for action:**
  - **IRRI:** "1 BPH per stem or less" leaves time to act if numbers rise. *(S6)*
  - **TNAU** gives an economic threshold level of "1 hopper/ tiller in the absence of predatory spider and 2 hoppers /tiller when spider is present at 1/hill". *(S9)*

  Both are field counts, which satellite and weather data cannot measure. Reaching them is a reason to contact the Department of Agriculture or KVK.
- **Prevention:**
  - keep fields and bunds free of weeds;
  - plant neighbouring fields within three weeks of each other;
  - avoid close planting;
  - use alternate wetting and drying;
  - avoid excess nitrogen;
  - conserve spiders and other natural enemies;
  - avoid indiscriminate insecticide use, which can trigger outbreaks. *(S2, S6, S9)*
